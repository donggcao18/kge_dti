\chapter{Experimental Setup and Results}
\label{chap:experiments}

\section{Experimental Setup}

The experiments evaluate drug--target interaction prediction on the Yamanishi08
dataset under two class-ratio settings: Yamanishi 1:1 and Yamanishi 1:10. Each
setting contains ten supplied folds. The same fold definitions are used by the
classical descriptor baselines and the KGE-NFM variants so that their average
ROC-AUC and PR-AUC values are directly comparable.

The classical baselines use the 1,024-dimensional Morgan fingerprint for each
drug and the CTD descriptor for each target protein. In the KGE-NFM pipeline,
the CTD block is reduced by PCA before it is combined with fold-specific entity
representations learned from the knowledge graph. DistMult and CompGCN are
evaluated as alternative KGE encoders, while the downstream NFM and evaluation
procedure are kept fixed.

provides the more demanding ranking setting. The resulting configuration is
then used for the principal comparison and the ablation analysis.

\section{Evaluation Metrics}

\subsection{ROC-AUC}

ROC-AUC measures the probability that a randomly selected positive interaction
is ranked above a randomly selected negative interaction. It summarizes ranking
quality over all classification thresholds, although it may appear optimistic
when the negative class is much larger than the positive class.

\subsection{PR-AUC}

PR-AUC summarizes the precision--recall curve and therefore emphasizes the
quality of positive predictions. It is particularly informative for Yamanishi
1:10, where false-positive predictions have a stronger effect on precision.

CompGCN-NFM achieves the best average value for both metrics in both settings.
On Yamanishi 1:1, it improves over DistMult-NFM by 0.0276 ROC-AUC and 0.0291
PR-AUC. On Yamanishi 1:10, the corresponding gains are smaller but remain
positive, at 0.0063 ROC-AUC and 0.0047 PR-AUC. Both KGE encoders therefore add
useful relational information beyond the descriptor-only baselines, while
CompGCN provides the strongest representation for the downstream NFM.

Among the classical models, Random Forest is the strongest baseline on
Yamanishi 1:10, reaching 0.9486 ROC-AUC and 0.7743 PR-AUC. CompGCN-NFM improves
these values by 0.0380 and 0.1619, respectively. The PR-AUC difference is
especially important because it shows that the proposed pipeline preserves
substantially higher precision under the more imbalanced class ratio.

The transition from Yamanishi 1:1 to Yamanishi 1:10 affects PR-AUC much more
strongly than ROC-AUC. Logistic Regression falls from 0.6613 to 0.1726 PR-AUC,
and the two tree-based baselines also decline. In contrast, DistMult-NFM and
CompGCN-NFM retain PR-AUC values above 0.93. This contrast supports the use of
knowledge graph representations and non-linear feature interaction modeling
when the candidate set contains many more negative pairs.

\section{Hyperparameter Tuning}
\label{sec:hyperparameter_tuning}

Hyperparameter tuning is conducted on Yamanishi 1:10. The recorded experiments
follow a staged search over the embedding dimension, number of negative samples,
and number of CompGCN layers. Parameters not identified in a configuration name
are kept at the corresponding run defaults.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{CompGCN hyperparameter tuning results on Yamanishi 1:10}
\label{tab:compgcn_tuning}
\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{Xcc}
\toprule
\textbf{Recorded configuration} & \textbf{ROC-AUC} & \textbf{PR-AUC} \\
\midrule
Embedding dimension 200 & 0.986336 & 0.932113 \\
Embedding dimension 400 & 0.980704 & 0.889596 \\
Embedding dimension 600 & 0.965040 & 0.839139 \\
Embedding 200, 3 negatives & 0.985824 & 0.932002 \\
Embedding 200, 5 negatives & 0.986321 & 0.933304 \\
Embedding 200, 5 negatives, 1 layer & 0.985215 & 0.927070 \\
Embedding 200, 5 negatives, 3 layers & \textbf{0.986632} & \textbf{0.936223} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

The dimension search favors 200-dimensional embeddings. Increasing the
dimension to 400 and 600 reduces both metrics, with PR-AUC decreasing from
0.932113 to 0.889596 and 0.839139. This result suggests that a larger embedding
space adds unnecessary model capacity for the available graph and training
data rather than improving the interaction representation.

With the embedding dimension fixed at 200, five negative samples produce a
higher PR-AUC than three negative samples. The layer comparison then shows that
three CompGCN layers outperform one layer, raising PR-AUC from 0.927070 to
0.936223. Consequently, the selected configuration uses a 200-dimensional
embedding, five negative samples per positive triple, and three CompGCN layers.

\section{Ablation Study}
\label{sec:ablation_study}

The ablation study is performed on Yamanishi 1:10 using the selected CompGCN
configuration. It separates the effect of the standalone KGE prediction,
descriptor-based NFM prediction, dense KGE embeddings, and the complete
multimodal pipeline. The full CompGCN-NFM result from
Table~\ref{tab:main_results} is included as the reference configuration.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Ablation results on Yamanishi 1:10}
\label{tab:ablation_study}
\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{Xrrrr}
\toprule
\textbf{Configuration} & \textbf{ROC-AUC} & \textbf{PR-AUC} &
\textbf{$\Delta$ ROC vs. full} & \textbf{$\Delta$ PR vs. full} \\
\midrule
KGE only & 0.619957 & 0.145284 & $-0.366675$ & $-0.790939$ \\
NFM only, without KGE dense embeddings & 0.929957 & 0.845284 & $-0.056675$ & $-0.090939$ \\
Without Morgan and protein descriptors & 0.969570 & 0.922840 & $-0.017062$ & $-0.013383$ \\
Full CompGCN-NFM & \textbf{0.986632} & \textbf{0.936223} & 0.000000 & 0.000000 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

The standalone KGE scorer performs poorly, particularly in PR-AUC, which shows
that graph scoring alone is insufficient for the final binary prediction task.
The NFM-only configuration is substantially stronger, achieving 0.929957
ROC-AUC and 0.845284 PR-AUC without the dense KGE embedding vectors. This
confirms that the descriptor fields and NFM interaction mechanism provide a
strong predictive foundation. Adding the KGE dense embeddings in the full model
improves ROC-AUC by 0.0567 and PR-AUC by 0.0909, with the larger PR-AUC gain
showing that graph-derived features are particularly useful for ranking positive
interactions under the 1:10 class ratio.

Removing both the Morgan fingerprint and protein CTD descriptor produces a
smaller reduction. The remaining KGE-NFM configuration reaches 0.969570
ROC-AUC and 0.922840 PR-AUC, corresponding to losses of 0.0171 and 0.0134 from
the full model. Thus, the KGE embeddings and NFM interaction layer account for
most of the predictive performance, while the molecular and sequence
descriptors provide a smaller but consistent complementary improvement. The
complete model remains strongest because it combines both representation
sources rather than relying on either source alone.

\section{Discussion}

The principal result is the consistent ordering of the average scores:
CompGCN-NFM performs best, followed by DistMult-NFM, while the classical
descriptor baselines remain behind the KGE-enhanced models. The improvement of
CompGCN over DistMult is larger on Yamanishi 1:1, but CompGCN also retains the
best ROC-AUC and PR-AUC on Yamanishi 1:10. This supports the methodological
motivation for relation-aware message passing: neighborhood information can
refine entity representations beyond the multiplicative interaction captured
by DistMult.

The difference between ROC-AUC and PR-AUC across the two settings also
illustrates why both metrics are required. Several models obtain a higher
ROC-AUC on Yamanishi 1:10 than on Yamanishi 1:1 even though their PR-AUC falls.
ROC-AUC therefore does not fully expose the loss of precision caused by the
larger negative class. The comparatively small PR-AUC reduction of the two
KGE-NFM models indicates better false-positive control under class imbalance.

The tuning and ablation results lead to two additional observations. First,
larger embeddings are not automatically beneficial; the 200-dimensional model
outperforms the 400- and 600-dimensional alternatives. Second, neither the
standalone KGE scorer nor the descriptor-based NFM fully matches the complete
pipeline. The strongest result is obtained when graph embeddings and biological
descriptors are combined in the downstream interaction model.

\section{Limitations}

The reported results have several limitations. First, the summary CSV contains
fold averages but not the fold-specific values required for confidence
intervals or paired significance testing. These values can be presented in
Figure~\ref{fig:fold_specific_candle_results} when the final candle chart is
added. Second, the tuning experiments form a staged search rather than an
exhaustive exploration of all parameter combinations. Third, unobserved
drug--target pairs are treated as negatives even though some may represent
interactions that have not yet been experimentally confirmed. Finally, the
current comparison covers the two Yamanishi class-ratio settings and does not
by itself establish generalization to entirely unseen drugs or proteins.
