\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Drug--target interaction (DTI) prediction aims to identify likely associations between drug molecules and biological targets such as proteins, enzymes, and receptors. Reliable DTI evidence supports drug repurposing, therapeutic mechanism analysis, side-effect investigation, and candidate prioritization \cite{ezzat2019computational}. Experimental screening remains essential, but it is expensive and cannot exhaustively evaluate the large space of possible drug--target pairs. Computational models therefore provide a practical way to rank candidate interactions before laboratory validation.

Classical DTI methods often represent the task as supervised learning over drug and protein descriptors, such as molecular fingerprints and sequence-derived protein descriptors \cite{rogers2010extended,dubchak1995prediction}. The benchmark study of Yamanishi et al. framed the problem by integrating chemical and genomic spaces \cite{yamanishi2008prediction}, and this view remains influential for descriptor-based prediction. However, biomedical entities are also connected through heterogeneous relations involving diseases, pathways, biological processes, side effects, protein families, and protein--protein interactions. These relations form a biomedical knowledge graph (KG), whose structure can provide evidence that is not present in isolated molecular or sequence features.

Knowledge graph embedding (KGE) provides one way to represent this relational context. A KGE model maps biomedical entities into dense vectors learned from graph triples, so the representation of a drug or protein can reflect not only direct DTI observations but also auxiliary relations in the surrounding graph \cite{bordes2013translating,yang2015embedding}. These graph-derived vectors can then be used together with descriptor features in a supervised DTI model.

This report studies a KGE-NFM pipeline in which drug and protein KG embeddings are combined with Morgan fingerprints for drugs and CTD descriptors for proteins. The downstream predictor is a neural factorization machine (NFM), which is suitable for modeling interactions among heterogeneous feature fields \cite{he2017neural,ye2021unified}. In this setting, NFM is used to combine graph-derived, molecular, and sequence-derived information for binary DTI classification.

The comparison focuses on two KG encoders under the same downstream NFM protocol. DistMult provides an efficient bilinear KGE baseline \cite{yang2015embedding}, while CompGCN provides a relation-aware message-passing encoder that composes entity and relation representations before aggregation \cite{vashishth2020composition}. The goal is to examine whether the CompGCN-based representation improves downstream DTI prediction relative to the DistMult-based configuration when the descriptor fields, evaluation protocol, and NFM classifier are held fixed.
